03 // DEFINITION TERMINOLOGY

Digital Emergency Health

Definition:

The application of Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) to provide immediate medical assistance, diagnostic accuracy, and care coordination during acute health crises or accidents.

APA Citation Reference:

World Health Organization. (2020). Global strategy on digital health 2020-2025. World Health Organization. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240010598

Academic Explanation:

Digital Emergency Health serves as the vital bridge between static medical records and dynamic trauma response. It emphasizes the "Glanceability" of data, ensuring that first responders can act upon validated patient histories within the "Golden Hour," thereby reducing the risk of medical errors caused by information asymmetry.

04 // CH 1 INTRODUCTION

The Digital Health Shift

Universal Health Coverage (UHC) is increasingly dependent on digital infrastructure.

Bangladesh has 98% NID penetration, yet emergency healthcare remains paper-reliant. Backend systems are robust, but the **Adoption Void** persists due to a neglected human-interface layer.

Systemic Failure

Sophisticated backend systems fail when the "Human Interface" is ignored in life-critical moments.

05 // THE PROBLEM 4 SYSTEMIC GAPS

Core Research Challenges

1. Time Crisis

Manual data verification causes 48% of delays during the "Golden Hour".

2. Trust Deficit

53.3% of surveyed users fear data breaches; 43.3% worry about comorbidity misuse.

3. Intimidation Gap

Complex UIs designed for tech-natives create paralysis for elderly users (59+).

4. Clarity Gap

Existing digital records lack medical "glanceability" required in trauma zones.
