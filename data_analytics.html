Chapter 01
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background History

Global Paradigm vs. Local Reality

Systemic Foundation

Digital technologies are foundational for Universal Health Coverage (UHC). Bangladesh has 98% NID penetration, yet emergency care remains paper-reliant.

Historical Blindness

Absence of a digital health dataset means responders cannot recall medical history, leading to lethal errors in acute care.

The Adoption Void

Sophisticated backend ecosystems fail when the "Human Interface" is ignored.

1.2 Research Overview

The Retrieval Bridge

Activation

Immediate response activation through passive identifiers.

Data Sync

Seamless synchronization between NID and clinical databases.

Life Action

Minimizing triage time to maximize survival in the Golden Hour.

1.3 Problem Statement

Socio-Technical Gaps

Problem 1

Acute info blindness in the Golden Hour. 48% of delays are manual process-related.

Problem 2

Regional Surveillance Fear prevents adoption of digital health identifiers.

Problem 3

Complexity leads to navigation paralysis for non-tech users and seniors (59+).

1.4 Research Objectives

Objectives (RO)

RO1

Identify specific needs and HCD requirements for Bangladesh.

RO2

Develop the DEHCMsync validated UI/UX framework.

RO3

Evaluate impact on satisfaction and retrieval speed.

1.5 Research Questions

Inquiries (RQ)

RQ1: What HCD requirements minimize retrieval time for low digital literacy users?

RQ2: How can "Transparent Security" UI bridge intimidation and trust gaps?

RQ3: Does the framework improve usage satisfaction for citizens with limited tech exposure?

1.6 Inclusion

Empowering the Elderly

Design Pillar

Digital equity is achieved only when the most vulnerable can navigate health systems without assistance.

Visual Aids

High-contrast modes and oversized typography.

Assisted Navigation

Simplified flows for motor-control decline.

Accessible by Default

Universal Design for Human Life
