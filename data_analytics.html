INTRODUCTION

05 // CH 1BACKGROUND

Global Paradigm vs. Local Reality

Systemic Foundation:

Digital technologies are foundational for Universal Health Coverage (UHC). Bangladesh has 98% NID penetration, yet emergency care remains paper-reliant.

Historical Blindness:

Absence of a digital health dataset means responders cannot recall any medical history of that patient, leading to lethal errors in acute care.

The Adoption Void

Sophisticated backend ecosystems fail when the "Human Interface" is ignored.

06 // PROBLEM STATEMENT

The Research Problem

Bridging the Socio-Technical Divide

Despite significant technical infrastructure in Bangladesh, emergency healthcare faces a critical failure at the human interaction layer. This results in information blindness during the "Golden Hour," directly increasing mortality rates and excluding vulnerable elderly populations who struggle with existing complex digital interfaces.

Primary Concern

The disconnect between high-end backend data systems and the chaotic, high-pressure reality of a trauma responder at the point of care.

User Impact

59+ demographic faces interaction paralysis due to UI clutter, leading to total abandonment of life-saving digital tools.

07 // RPGAP ANALYSIS

1. Time Crisis (Emergency Gap)

Information blindness during the "Golden Hour" results in 48% of avoidable clinical delays due to manual verification.

2. Trust Deficit (Privacy Gap)

Deep surveillance anxieties prevent 53.3% of users from enrolling in digital portals without visual security cues.

3. Intimidation Gap (Usability)

Complex, technocentric UIs create navigation paralysis for elderly users (59+) and low-literate demographic groups.

4. Clarity Gap (Visualization)

Unstructured data dumps lack medical glanceability, preventing paramedics from understanding patient risks in seconds.

RQ1: Requirements

What specific human-centered design requirements are essential to minimize data retrieval time in emergencies?

RQ2: Integration

How can accessibility principles and cultural trust markers be effectively integrated into a cohesive framework?

RQ3: Improvement

To what extent does the proposed framework improve usability metrics (efficiency, errors) for non-tech-savvy users?

14 // CH 3APPROACH

Research Approach: The HCD Path

ISO 9241-210 Standards

Study follows an Iterative Design Life Cycle focusing on the context of use. It balances medical urgency with physiological elderly decline.

Multi-Phase Framework

Combines Thematic Inquiry (Qualitative) and Heuristic Prototyping with final Empirical Validation (Quantitative).

Phase 1: Discovery
  • User Interviews
  • Friction Mapping
  • Contextual Inquiry
Phase 2: Prototyping
  • UI Visualization
  • DEHCMsync Logic
  • Trust Anchor Design
Phase 3: Validation
  • Thinking Aloud
  • SUS Benchmarking
  • Expert Review
15 // ANALYSISTECHNIQUES

Data Triangulation & Analysis

Qualitative: Thematic Coding

  • Transcription of interviews (N=20).
  • NVivo deductive coding for Cognitive Load.
  • Identification of Surveillance Barriers.

Quantitative: Empirical Metrics

  • SUS (System Usability Scale) scoring.
  • Comparative Error-Rate benchmarking.
  • Paramedic Time-on-Task (Target < 30s).

Cross-validation ensures that the resulting framework is both statistically significant and humanly inclusive.
