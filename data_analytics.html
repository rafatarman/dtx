Chapter 01
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background

Global Paradigm vs. Local Reality

Systemic Foundation

Digital technologies are foundational for Universal Health Coverage (UHC). Bangladesh has 98% NID penetration, yet emergency care remains paper-reliant.

Historical Blindness

Absence of a digital health dataset means responders cannot recall medical history, leading to lethal errors in acute care.

The Adoption Void: Sophisticated systems fail when the "Human Interface" is ignored.

1.2 Challenges

Socio-Technical Gaps

Problem 1: Time

Acute info blindness in the Golden Hour. 48% of delays are caused by manual paperwork processes.

Problem 2: Trust

Regional Surveillance Fear prevents adoption of digital health identifiers despite technical maturity.

Problem 3: UI

Technocentric complexity leads to navigation paralysis for non-tech users and seniors (59+).

1.3 Objectives

Research Objectives (RO)

RO1

Identify user needs and HCD requirements for the Bangladeshi context.

RO2

Develop the DEHCMsync validated UI/UX framework integrating trust cues.

RO3

Evaluate framework impact on satisfaction and retrieval speed benchmarks.

1.4 Questions

Research Questions (RQ)

RQ1: Design Logic

What HCD requirements minimize retrieval time for users with limited literacy?

RQ2: Cultural Trust

How can "Transparent Security" UI cues and age-friendly markers bridge trust gaps?

RQ3: Usability

Does the framework improve usage satisfaction for citizens with tech-anxiety?

1.5 Inclusivity

Empowering the Elderly

Design Pillar

Digital equity is achieved only when the most vulnerable can navigate health systems without assistance.

Visual Aids

High-contrast modes and oversized typography.

Assisted Navigation

Simplified flows for motor-control decline.

Accessible by Default

Universal Design for Human Life
